


Polygons are passé: how Individual Tree Crown (ITC) mapping technology
will inform future forest management


From local to national to global levels, improvements in the accuracy, 
timeliness and cost-effectiveness of forest information are badly needed.
Most forest inventories in Canada are still produced largely through the 
interpretation of aerial/satellite images anchored by field information, 
a tedious and costly process. 

Instead, it could be more useful to use computers to delineate individual tree crowns, 
assess their species and then regroup them into forest stands (if needed).
Alternatively, databases of properly located and recognized individual trees
could change our ways of managing the forest, minimizing the differences between 
management and operational inventories.

This talk will describe the fundamentals of the ITC-Suite and its numerous applications.
